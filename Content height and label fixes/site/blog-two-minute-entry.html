The task you keep moving needs a smaller door

Every list has one: the item that has moved five times, still matters, and still has not started. Moving it a sixth time will not help.

A task that keeps being rescheduled is usually neither too big nor unimportant. It is undefined. “Sort out the insurance” has no first move, so every time you look at it you have to do the thinking before you can do the work — and thinking is what you have least of at the moment you are choosing.

What the entry is

An entry is the first two-minute step, written down as part of the task. Not a subtask list, not a plan: one action small enough that refusing it is embarrassing. Open the artwork and pick the print side. Export the card statement. Find the policy number.

Two minutes is not arbitrary. It is short enough to survive a bad morning, and long enough to leave the task in a different state than it was — and a task in a different state is a task whose next step you can see.

Why it belongs on the block, not in the detail view

An entry hidden behind a click is an entry nobody reads at the moment it would help. So it sits on the block in the day, as the one thing on it built as a button: pressing it starts the work. Everything else on a block is read; this is the only part that is pressed, and it looks like it.

The counter this replaces

The usual answer to a stuck task is to break it into parts and watch a counter. Parts are useful — Needt has them, one level deep — but a counter is a measure, not a door. Nought of five is an accurate description of a task you still have not started.

When Needt writes it for you

After the second reschedule the app offers an entry. It is the one place a model earns its keep here: taking a vague title and naming a concrete first action. You can rewrite it, and the version you write is the one that sticks — the point is having one, not having ours.
