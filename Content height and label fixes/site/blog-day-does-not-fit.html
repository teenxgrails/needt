When a day does not fit

Eight hours of work will go into a four-hour day in every task app ever made. The list accepts it without comment, and you find out at six.

A to-do list has no opinion about time. You can put forty items on it and it will render forty items, each of them equally weightless. That is not a bug in any particular app — it is what a list is. The cost lands later, in the evening, when the difference between what you intended and what the day could hold becomes a mood rather than a number.

The arithmetic nobody does

The calculation is not hard. Take the hours that are actually free — not the hours between nine and six, but what is left after the meetings you already agreed to. Subtract what is already promised to something. Compare the remainder to the estimates of what is due. The answer is a single number, and it is either positive or it is not.

Doing that by hand once a day is tedious enough that almost nobody does it, which is exactly the kind of work software should take. In Needt the columns state it without being asked: 5 h 20 min of work against 4 h free. Not a warning banner — a fact in the header, in the same type as everything else.

Capacity is what is left of today, not what a day holds

One detail decides whether this is useful or merely decorative: at half past one, today does not have eight hours in it. It has four and a half. A planner that measures a day against its full length will tell you a day fits right up to the point where it obviously does not.

Saying what slips, before you press anything

Knowing the day is oversubscribed is half an answer. The other half is which things move — and that has to be visible before you accept the fix, not after. Needt shows the specific tasks that would leave the day, with where they would land, and then waits. Reschedule and it happens; undo and it is as it was.

This is the same principle as the agent cursor: a change you did not watch happen is a change you have to verify by hand, which costs more than doing it yourself.

What this does not solve

It does not make the work smaller. If your week genuinely contains more than your week, no arrangement of blocks fixes that, and a tool that implies otherwise is lying pleasantly. What it does is move the discovery from the evening to the morning, when you can still decide what not to do.
