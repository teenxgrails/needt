Comparisons

Most tools are one half of the problem.

Not a feature table. A feature table hides the thing that actually decides whether a tool works for you, which is what it is a tool for.

Against a to-do app

A list is weightless by design: forty items render as forty items. It will never tell you the day does not fit, because it does not know how long the day is. Needt keeps the list — and then makes each item occupy real time, which is where the decisions are.

Choose the list app if you want a fast inbox and nothing else. Choose Needt if the problem is the day, not the capture.

Against a calendar

A calendar knows exactly how long the day is and nothing about what is outstanding. Everything in it was agreed with someone else; your own work has to be typed in as fake meetings and rebuilt by hand every time something moves. Needt puts tasks and events on one grid and rebuilds the placement on request.

Keep your calendar. Needt reads it rather than replacing it.

Against a project tool

Boards, stages and dependencies are built for a team's throughput, not for one person's Tuesday. They answer “where is this work” and never “what do I do at two o'clock”. Needt has the board and the dependency flow, but they exist to answer the second question.

Choose the project tool if several people need one view. Choose Needt if the person who has to do the work is you.

Against an AI assistant

An assistant that writes a plan in prose leaves you to transcribe it, and one that rearranges your week without showing you costs more in verification than it saves. The Needt agent works the day in front of you, and every move is visible and reversible.

The arithmetic is arithmetic. The model writes the first step and the weekly summary — and shows you both before they are kept.
