Time blocking, honestly

Everyone who tries time blocking reports the same two weeks: it works, and then it stops. The method is not the problem.

Time blocking is the practice of giving every piece of work a place in the day rather than a place on a list. It works for a reason that is hard to argue with: a task with a time has to fit, and a task that has to fit forces the decision you were avoiding.

Why it collapses on day eleven

Not because of discipline. Because of maintenance. The first version of the day is cheap to make and worth making; the fourth version, at half past three, after two things moved and one ran long, is expensive and feels pointless. So people stop rebuilding it — and within a week the blocks on the calendar are fiction, which is worse than an empty calendar, because you stop believing the parts that were true.

The part that should be automatic

The judgement is yours: what matters, what can wait, what you refuse. The arithmetic is not: which hours are free, what still needs doing, what depends on what, which deadline is close enough to matter. Rebuilding the day from those inputs is mechanical, and mechanical work is what you delegate.

That is the whole claim behind Needt. Press one thing and the remaining work goes back into the hours that are genuinely open, with what you pinned untouched. The eleventh day costs the same as the first.

Three rules that keep it honest

What you agreed to is immovable. A meeting is not a preference. The scheduler plans around events and around blocks you pinned, and the rail on every block says which is which — coloured means it placed it and may move it again, grey means it may not.

Height is duration. A block drawn taller than its length reads as an event that runs longer than it does, so a Needt block is exactly as tall as it is long, and drops what it says rather than growing.

Nothing lands unseen. Every placement is drawn before it is kept. That is what makes a rebuilt day trustworthy rather than something to audit.

When not to bother

If your day is genuinely reactive — support, operations, small children — blocking the whole of it is theatre. Block the two or three things that will not survive being optional and leave the rest open. A planner should hold that arrangement without complaining: a day with three blocks and five free hours is a legitimate day, not an incomplete one.
